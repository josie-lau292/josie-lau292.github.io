---
title: Population vs Sample
excerpt: The whole group we care about, and the smaller group that helps us learn about it.
date: '2026-01-10'
readTime: 3 min read
category: statistics
---

A **population** is the full group you want to understand. It might be all first-year psychology students in Australia, all adolescents attending a service, or every result a program could produce.

A **sample** is the smaller group you can actually observe. We use a sample because collecting information from every person is often impossible, expensive, or slow.

The important question is not simply “how big is the sample?” It is also: *does it give us a reasonable picture of the population?* A carefully chosen smaller sample can be more useful than a large but narrow one.

When you read a finding, try asking: “Who was included—and who might be missing?” That one question makes statistics much more meaningful.

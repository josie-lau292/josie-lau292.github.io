---
title: Correlation Is Not Causation
excerpt: Two things moving together is interesting—but it is only the beginning of a question.
date: '2026-01-31'
readTime: 5 min read
category: statistics
---

A **correlation** tells us whether two variables tend to vary together. If one tends to be higher when the other is higher, that is a positive correlation. If one tends to be lower, that is a negative correlation.

But correlation alone cannot tell us that one variable caused the other. There may be a third factor involved, or the relationship may work in the other direction.

For example, ice-cream sales and sunburn both rise in summer. Ice cream does not cause sunburn; warmer weather helps explain both patterns.

Correlations are still valuable. They can show us where to look next, help us form useful questions, and guide more careful research designs.
